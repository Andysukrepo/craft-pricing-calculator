Help & Guides

Materials & bulk purchases

Divide pack price by pack quantity, convert compatible units, then multiply by the quantity needed plus waste. For example, a £20 pack of 1,000 g and 75 g used costs £1.50 before waste. Buying more cheaply per unit does not make unused stock free.

Labour & packaging

Multiply your hourly rate by total making minutes divided by 60. Include setup and finishing once. Count boxes, tissue and labels in either materials or packaging, not both.

Overheads & underpricing

Allocate running costs across a realistic number of products. Excluding rent, machinery or software can overstate estimated profit. A price below break-even does not cover your entered costs.

Marketplace fees & Etsy

Fee presets are editable estimates, not official rates. Verify your current agreement, advertising costs and whether fees apply to customer-paid postage. Split fixed order fees across the items in that order.

Margin, markup & profit

Margin divides profit by net revenue; markup divides profit by cost. A £5 cost and £10 price gives 50% margin and 100% markup before fees. Sales revenue is not profit.

VAT & wholesale

If VAT registered, £12 including VAT at 20% is £10 before output VAT and £2 output VAT. Only subtract recoverable input VAT you can substantiate. Wholesale discounts still need to cover costs, fees and your chosen target margin.

Fictional worked example

Illustration only: £1.50 materials + £5.00 maker time + £0.50 packaging + £0.50 allocated overhead = £7.50 production per item. At a £15 sale price and £1.50 selling fees, estimated profit is £6.00, or 40% of the £15 revenue. Ten items have £75 production cost. Real costs and tax treatment may differ.
